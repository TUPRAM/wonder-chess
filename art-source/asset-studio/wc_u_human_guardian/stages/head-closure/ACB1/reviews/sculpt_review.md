## Final recommendation

**PARKED_ART_REVISE.** Correction 2 is the better executed sculpt experiment because it removes most of correction 1's new stroke ridge. It does not establish a convincing corrected anatomical target or close the remaining likeness defects. Preserve correction 2 as experiment/evidence and retain baseline/initial as clean recovery sources. Do not reconstruct, project, or mirror a production surface from this unaccepted target. The final bounded target correction has been used; no additional stroke pass is recommended in this run.

## Observed progression

The initial target is visually close to baseline in the inspected directions; the changes are too small to establish a persuasive new anatomical solution.

Correction 1 introduces a clear major regression: a horizontal raised ridge/step under the right eye. In front, three-quarter, primary-fit, reversed-key, and underside views, the under-eye surface reads as a raised rectangular pad ending in a bar. A smaller hard mark appears on the nasal sidewall in profile. This is a stroke boundary, not a convincing cheekbone or natural lower-lid transition. Reversing the light confirms that the feature persists beyond one lighting arrangement.

Correction 2 removes most of this hard stripe and softens the smaller sidewall mark. This is useful recovery from correction 1. There is also modest added upper-muzzle support near the nasal base. However, recovery from a newly introduced defect must not be counted as establishing improvement over the original baseline. In the final matched views, the defining problems remain unresolved.

## Why this sculpt target is not ready to drive reconstruction

The visible edits are local deposits and removal of their stroke edges, without a sufficiently clear anatomical volume replacing the previous transition. Correction 1 added a short high-contrast boundary across the cheek. Correction 2 made that boundary softer, but softening the boundary did not create the intended cheek/socket turn. Similarly, the small nasal-base addition changed local fullness without establishing the complete columella-to-philtrum-to-upper-lip cross-section. This is an inference from the observed shape progression, not a claim that native sculpting itself is unsuitable.

A reconstructed cage would reproduce these remaining pad and shelf forms if this target were treated as authoritative. Park the target. A future focused intervention should provide one visibly corrected connected orbital/upper-cheek and nasal-base/upper-muzzle volume before any projection or cage rebuilding, with the same exact view set. It should preserve the useful opening and fixed outer boundary and distinguish an anatomical shape change from another smoothness change. Any renewed attempt requires the next bounded task or explicit focused intervention; this review does not authorize it.
