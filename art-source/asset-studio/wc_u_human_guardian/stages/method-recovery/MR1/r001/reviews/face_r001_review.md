## Visible improvement

The candidate has a genuine aperture: the opening remains when the globe is hidden. The uniform-clay globe does not carry the baseline's embossed circular relief. The broad lower-lid/upper-cheek area is fuller and substantially more continuous than the baseline's repeated horizontal cheek bands. The aperture also reads as an independently curved upper and lower boundary rather than a circle cut into a spherical crater. The lower boundary is shallower than the upper arc.

These improvements are visible in the rendered images and remain useful under reversed lighting. They justify retaining this candidate as a better starting point for the local method.

## Critical remaining relation

The **screen-right aperture corner** is the clearest residual failure. This is an image-space identification because the supplied images do not label anatomical side or show a centerline.

- In `face_r001_gaze_front.png`, the right end of the aperture finishes as a small tucked angular cut rather than a clearly readable corner turn.
- In `face_r001_clay_three_quarter.png` and `face_r001_opening_three_quarter.png`, the end is comparatively upright and abruptly rounded, with a short horizontal crease extending away from the corner into the surrounding skin.
- The corner crease remains attached to that location in `face_r001_reverse_three_quarter.png`; its shading changes, but the relation remains visible. This is evidence of a persistent form/normal issue, not proof of a particular mesh cause.
- `face_r001_gaze_opposite.png` exposes a pointed projection at the corresponding corner beside the globe. The isolated open shell naturally exposes its rear, but that does not establish a clean lid-corner construction.
- Across the front and three-quarter clay views, the lid margin/body distinction remains weak. Much of the rim reads as a thin aperture edge in a smooth sheet. A narrow thickness cue exists along portions of the lower edge, but the turn at the flagged corner is not adequately resolved.

## One bounded correction

Edit the flagged corner and its neighboring lid-margin and first socket-transition rows as a local group. Establish a finite corner turn with a controlled edge thickness, remove the short outward crease/pointed projection, and fade into the existing fuller upper cheek. Keep the successful cheek volume and the independent upper/lower aperture arcs. Do not add broad smoothing or shrink the whole region onto the globe to address this local failure.

Recapture the same front, profile, both three-quarter, hidden-globe clay, and reversed-light views. Improvement means the corner turns continuously in those views, retains an actual opening and credible globe relation, and does not exchange the small corner defect for a new lower-lid trough or round surprised aperture.
