## Primary blocker: facial transition geometry

The head has strong serrated transitions around the regional replacement perimeter. They form a V across the lower forehead, run down both sides of the nose, and extend across the lower cheeks toward the ears. The individual teeth and abrupt plane changes are visible at ordinary head framing in front, profile and both oblique views. They persist under reversed light. The nasal segments are especially severe: repeated triangular steps interrupt the nose-to-cheek relationship and create deep alternating light/dark notches.

The baseline has its own eye relief and horizontal cheek bands, but it does not have this stitched-looking perimeter. The contextual assembly therefore introduces a major integration defect even though the central eye and upper-cheek study improves those earlier local failures.

`context_join_wire_three_quarter.png` visibly places the narrow triangulated bridge along the same defective perimeter. Its repeating unequal triangle groups correspond to the serrated transition. `context_source_cage_three_quarter.png` shows the lower-density regional cages before that bridge. These images support a failure at the regional-to-head transition; they do not alone prove an open boundary or establish the exact computational cause.

### Connectivity clarification and correction of preliminary wording

The modeling owner reports the derived head has **0 degenerate faces, 0 nonmanifold internal edges, and 80 boundary edges at the two eye apertures**, with a **460-triangle connected bridge between 68 regional and 392 head boundary vertices**. These are owner-reported technical checks, not independently repeated by this reviewer.

Accordingly, the preliminary image-only description of the facial seams as possibly open/overlapping must not be used as an established diagnosis. The confirmed visual finding is a severe geometric/shading transition failure **despite reported connectivity**. A connectivity pass does not resolve the visible serrations or make the assembly acceptable.

## Secondary contextual failures

**Expanded hair contact and gathering:** the added counter-sweep has a conspicuous dark gap/arched separation along its underside in front, three-quarter and top views. The prior single leading clump's accepted local contact does not prove this new mass is seated correctly. The rear gathering reads as separate leaf-like plates placed over the cap, with abrupt exposed upper edges and uneven overlap at the rear/top. It does not yet create a convincing flow from the crown into the braid.

**Hairstyle likeness:** broad masses are a useful improvement over the baseline's repeated tubes, but this assembly is still dominated by a smooth cap and a few isolated slabs. The approved crop's irregular sweep rhythm, temple curl and gathered transition are not yet present as a coherent hairstyle. The braid's upper/intermediate segments remain visibly repetitive. The initial tight framing clipped the tail; the full-framing addendum below confirms a tapered lower braid with a finite tie/tail, so tail absence is not a valid defect. No exact length or unseen rear-reference measurement is inferred from the crop, and the retained braid is frozen under the user's MR1 scope.

**Two-eye expression:** the diagnostic gaze shows both actual openings and avoids the old embossed eye relief. However, the paired gaze still reads wide and fixed, especially with the raised strip-like brow context. It does not yet establish the approved calm, firm expression. The severe facial seams and changed visible forehead/hair framing also prevent a clean likeness assessment. Expression tuning is lower priority than rebuilding the failed transition.

**Inherited face proportions:** the pointed wedge-like nose profile and simple lip/jaw construction are also present in the matched baseline. They are not attributed to the local proof as new regressions. Their suitability for the approved identity is not accepted here.

## Narrowest useful next intervention

Stop broad full-head passes on this assembly. Preserve the accepted eye/socket/cheek source and the accepted leading hair clump as separate usable studies, and retain the failed assembly as integration evidence.

For the next integration attempt, isolate **one short nasal-to-upper-cheek boundary segment on one recorded anatomical side**. Deliberately rebuild a transition band with compatible sampling and planned surface tangents on both the regional and retained-head sides. The owner's reported 68-to-392 boundary mismatch should be addressed through the local topology and vertex placement rather than bridged again around the whole ring and softened afterward. Keep the working eye aperture/lid rows and fuller cheek intact while resolving the segment.

The intervention is successful only when an unsmoothed cage and a modest subdivision/clay preview show a continuous nasal-cheek transition in front, profile, both oblique views and reversed light, without triangular teeth, a visible ledge or a new cheek trough. Then extend that proven boundary treatment around the remaining perimeter. If deliberate transition retopology cannot be executed with the present tool control, use the focused manual modeling handoff; do not restart the same global bridge/smoothing approach under a new revision label.

After the facial transition is proven, apply the already accepted root/underside method independently to the counter-sweep and one rear gathering junction. Do not add more locks or braid detail to disguise an unseated mass. Retain the frozen braid geometry; new complete-hair review framing must include the crown, rear gathering and existing finite braid tail.
