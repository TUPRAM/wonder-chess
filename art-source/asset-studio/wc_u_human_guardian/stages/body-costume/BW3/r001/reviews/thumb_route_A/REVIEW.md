## Visible findings

1. **The thumb base and thenar mass are substantially more continuous.** Compared with the onset's upright, strip-like thumb construction and the old BW2 long knife-fold, route A sweeps the thumb across the palm through a broad rounded base. The oblique handle-hidden image exposes that volume rather than allowing the cylinder to conceal it. This is a meaningful local improvement and should be preserved.
2. **The isolated side and axial silhouettes are more plausible.** The thumb remains a distinct projecting volume separated from the index-side wall of the hand. These views do not reproduce the long narrow slot descending through the old BW2 palm. The thumb is currently rather smooth and uniform, so joint/pad definition is still limited; that does not justify replacing the useful base shape during this contact-route study.
3. **A focal first-web dimple remains.** A small sharp V-shaped crease is visible where the thumb-side web meets the palm, especially in the oblique and axial views. It appears in both the glove and underlying skin. The glove is not independently producing this feature. It is much more localized than the old web collapse, but images alone cannot establish whether it is harmless compression, a weighting/pivot problem or a true surface crossing.

The earlier onset image and current views are not a numerically registered same-pose comparison. The current assessment is qualitative and local: different finger states, camera/framing and thumb pose preclude a quantified improvement score. The full grip, all-digit contact, motion stability and self-collision remain outside what these pictures prove.

## Recommended bounded direction

Retain the staged clearance/opposition route as the current visual basis while the independent numerical collision audit runs. There is no visual reason in these isolated images to discard the broader thenar mass or return to the BW2 thumb path.

Protect the new thumb-base clearance when pursuing contact. Do not chase distal-pad proximity by forcing the first web back into a thin fold. If the audit localizes unacceptable crossings at the visible V-dimple, diagnose the underlying skin's joint/weight response first; a glove-only cosmetic patch would repeat the feature already present below it. A next correction should broaden that local transition or adjust the permitted proximal relationship while preserving thumb length and the existing base volume. This recommendation is conditional on actual cause evidence, not permission for another global pose search.

No whole-hand enclosure, opposing-pad contact pass, collision freedom, continuous carrying, actual sword adaptation, second-body replay, recipe promotion or human approval is issued by this visual review.
