## Hand: retained correction1, hilt-only diagnostics

The four finger wraps are distinct and their continuous route around the hilt is easier to read. However, they are nearly uniform rounded rings with little differentiation between finger-base, proximal knuckle, middle joint and pad. In the palm view, long straight fan-like strips lead from the wrist to the finger roots instead of forming a substantial palm and a staggered knuckle arrangement. The oblique view makes the repeated ring construction particularly obvious.

The side and axial views show a large open channel around the hilt and a hook-shaped thumb. The thumb has not established convincing opposed pad contact or a rounded thenar/web transition into the palm. The wrist/palm extrusion is flat and flared, and the thumb root has a localized crease/kink. A successful exclusion channel or absence of crossings would not remove these major construction defects. No reliable millimeter distance is inferred from the images.

The hilt-only images do not show the guard or full sword. Their visual clearance cannot be independently approved from this subset. Anatomical digit naming/order likewise needs the source record; image position alone is not evidence of a handedness error.

Major hand defects:

1. Uniform ring-like fingers without convincing knuckles, pad shapes or staggered roots.
2. Broad thin fan at palm/wrist, with insufficient palm and thumb-base volume.
3. Hook-shaped thumb and visually loose grip/contact in axial and side views.
4. Unproven wrist/cuff integration and full-equipment clearance in the reviewed images.

## Torso: correction2 before the announced collar rollback

Against the reviewed BW4 source captures, the main plate is visibly shorter, less projecting in profile, and leaves a more deliberate waist-underlayer region. The backplate now has a raised central hem rather than extending almost to the belt. Those are real large-form changes, not merely different highlights.

The profile remains the strongest failure view: a thin upper edge/attachment strip bridges a substantial open wedge above the front plate, with the padded torso behind it. The armhole/side transition still reads as an edge hanging in space rather than a fitted return or believable fastening. The lower shell is improved but has not resolved the upper attachment system.

The correction2 collar has severe scalloping/serration and a flared, sharply kinked outline. Rejecting that collar is justified. This is a review of the images before rollback; it does not establish that the eventual restored collar is finished. The older collar also has an unresolved neck interface in the source views.

The new waist region is visibly covered in the central front/back views, but the front image still shows abrupt lateral ends and dark openings around the upper belt attachment. These images do not establish continuous side closure during motion. The exact collision status must come from the separately recorded geometric checks. The clay images do not establish a final navy material.

Major torso defects:

1. Upper side/rim and shoulder-attachment spacing remains too open in profile.
2. Correction2 collar is visibly failed and should not be included as a retained improvement.
3. Waist-side/belt closure still needs coherent construction and motion evidence.
4. Retained shoulder, bracer, lower costume and head remain context; the changed torso does not clear them.
