# 02 — Ada Brightshield: proposed visual rebuild

## Art goal

Create a polished, original, stylized high-fantasy **gate guardian**, not a faceted generic soldier and not a photorealistic cinematic character. She should feel capable, reassuring, and physically grounded. Her shield and stance explain her role before a tooltip appears.

Keep the current name, identity, unit ID, race/class, warm brown skin, dark eyes, square brows, short black braid, ivory coat, navy tabard, steel armor, restrained gold, short broad sword, and rising-sun shield. Keep gameplay unchanged. This document develops the existing brief; it does not replace Ada with a different character.

The current data describes a rounded shield, quilted coat, brushed steel, and a calm alert face. The new work must turn those words into visible structure rather than merely satisfying a list of object names.

## Three silhouette candidates before detail

Create three rough alternatives with the same equipment and identity. These are controlled art experiments, not additional game characters. Render front, three-quarter, and actual board-camera views with the same framing.

| Candidate | Primary idea | What changes | Risk to inspect |
|---|---|---|---|
| A — Welcoming Gatekeeper | Protective, sturdy, approachable | Broad shield-side shoulder; moderate head emphasis; planted legs; outward shield | Do not become a featureless toy or hide the face |
| B — Resolute Champion | More heroic and upright | Taller torso rhythm; confident chest/neck; cleaner diagonal sword line | Do not lose thumbnail readability or make her visually interchangeable with a warrior |
| C — Compact Bastion | A strong small-screen icon | Compact torso; larger readable shield/head relationship; stronger boots | Do not confuse her with the Dwarf rig or make the shield fill the whole unit cell |

**Recommended direction:** A, borrowing B's clear face and C's readable equipment scale. Pram selects a direction from actual images. If concept-image generation is unavailable, use intentionally rough Blender silhouette studies; do not call written prompts completed concept art.

The chosen concept becomes one locked reference sheet. Changes after approval require a small decision note and updated views, not uncontrolled drift between renders.

## Primary forms and proportions

Retain 1.82 m nominal height unless the live canonical data differs. Explore approximately 5.5–6.5 head-heights as a stylization range, not a compulsory ratio or permission to silently distort a working rig. The test is actual silhouette and deformation. Existing joint positions are a compatibility baseline; a required revision must be evaluated separately.

Build a coherent ribcage, pelvis, shoulders, elbows, wrists, knees, and ankles before armor. Arms must emerge naturally from the torso; equipment must not conceal unreviewed connection problems. Keep enough negative space between elbows, torso, sword, and shield to read the pose.

The shield-side silhouette should be broad and secure. The sword-side silhouette should permit an open elbow and clear attack arc. Purposeful asymmetry is better than equal polygonal shoulder boxes plus arbitrary decorations.

Reserve strong contrast and shape changes for the face, shield crest, sword edge, and shoulder contour. Lower legs and secondary straps should support those focal areas rather than compete with them.

## Head and expression

Rebuild the head as a coherent stylized surface with a readable forehead, brow ridge, cheeks, jaw, chin, and neck connection. Avoid a spherical head with facial elements placed on its outside.

Eyes should sit in supported eyelid shapes, with intentional eye direction. Favor calm attentive brows over a permanently blank expression. The mouth can remain simple; its placement, corner shape, and relationship to the cheeks matter more than extra rings of lip geometry.

The nose should emerge from the brow/cheek structure rather than look like a separate wedge. Keep warm-brown skin and do not change identity for an easier generic base mesh. The short braid is a deliberate cluster of hair masses with a clean silhouette, not a tiny spike above the head or noisy spaghetti strands.

No complex facial animation is required for this pass. A convincing neutral head with a restrained optional blink can be enough. Do not add a facial-rig project before establishing an appealing face.

## Armor and costume

**Shoulders:** Replace eight-sided cap-like volumes with designed curved shells. Use a dominant plate and one restrained overlapping lower section. Show the plate edge, attachment logic, and the arm emerging underneath. Bevels should catch light without making every plate look inflated.

**Chest:** Shape the breastplate over the ribcage, with a controlled central plane and side transitions. Expose a readable collar/coat boundary. Do not use an unsupported flat hexagonal slab as the entire torso design.

**Coat:** Ivory quilted fabric should have broad volume at the shoulder and elbow plus a few structural folds. Quilting belongs mainly in baked/painted information, with silhouette geometry only where it changes shape. Avoid hundreds of raised stitches invisible during play.

**Tabard:** Use real thickness where visible, shaped hems, and a split that clears the legs. A few broad folds should communicate gravity and attachment. Do not turn it into disconnected rigid hanging rectangles.

**Boots/bracers:** Give boots a sole, toe transition, and ankle construction. Bracers should fit the forearm and taper toward the wrist. Keep seams and material changes deliberate, not generated uniformly on every surface.

## Shield: the main identity object

The shield is a rounded kite with a convex face, not a flat door. It needs a designed perimeter, controlled thickness, readable top shoulders, a tapered lower point, and a continuous bevel.

Create a visible distinction between the steel edge, navy face, and restrained rising-sun crest. Keep the crest broad enough to read at small scale. Prefer a few coherent rays or a compact relief to a clutter of little rods. Do not add a new functional blade, turret, or second magic mechanism to the shield.

Model the back: grip placement, straps, and clear forearm space. Make its construction plausible in the actual guard animation. The top edge must stay below her eyes during idle; during the active it may rise without permanently hiding the whole head.

The exact curvature is approved from front, side, and three-quarter views. Silhouette and highlight continuity matter more than an arbitrary segment target.

## Sword: a forged object, not an extruded outline

Retain a short broad fantasy sword. Give the blade a real cross-section: central ridge or clearly shaped main planes, edge bevels, taper toward the tip, and a restrained thickness change from base to tip.

The guard should transition into the grip and blade shoulder. Show a compact pommel and a usable grip length. A fuller is optional only when it remains readable; do not use engraving as a substitute for blade construction.

Check the blade at oblique angles. It must not turn into a flat panel or show unexplained shading bands. Keep its size and attack arc compatible with the board and animation.

## Hands and grip

The provided closeup reads as stacked loops around a handle. Replace that reading with one palm mass, knuckle rhythm, tapered curled fingers, and an opposing thumb.

Model the grip pose around the actual handle. Finger details need not become independently animated if the design does not require it. A simplified coherent gloved hand can be an explicit approved alternative, but do not silently change exposed skin to gloves just to hide defects.

Spend geometry where it changes the silhouette, hand/handle contact, or deformation. Do not allocate dense fingers by default while preserving a coarse head and angular shoulders. Inspect palm/forearm integration and the guard pressing into fingers at the whole animation's extrema.

## Palette and material direction

Preserve the canonical base palette: navy `#25466B`, ivory `#E9E2CD`, steel `#9EAAB5`, gold `#C7A25B`, plus her established skin/hair colors. These are reference swatches, not a requirement to flatten every surface into four uniform chips.

Steel should show controlled highlight shapes and modest wear around exposed edges. Cloth should be softer and quieter. Leather should look thicker and darker than fabric. Skin must not share the same specular response as armor. Gold should remain concentrated around the crest and two fasteners.

Use broad material variation, painted color accents, and actual baked details where useful. Avoid noisy photorealistic dirt, glowing seams everywhere, highly reflective chrome, or thick comic outlines added solely to disguise bad geometry.

## Pose and animation personality

Idle: rear-foot weight, steady head, shield angled away from the face, sword relaxed but ready. Avoid rigid symmetry.

Move: measured steps with readable weight transfer; no root-motion dependence added to gameplay.

Attack: a clear preparation, one short sweep, contact moment, and controlled recovery. Keep hand contact and shoulder clearance.

Active: a deliberate shield lift and crest activation. The effect follows the skill state; it does not decide damage/shield logic.

Hit/defeat/victory: preserve current semantic behavior and durations unless a reviewed presentation adjustment is necessary. Each must look intentional at gameplay scale, not only as one selected still.

Review generated views for consistency. A convincing image is not automatically a valid turnaround; reconcile discrepancies before modeling.

## Approval conditions

Ada should read as the same recognizable guardian in gray clay, color, silhouette, and animation. Pram approves the actual reference before the style propagates. 'Looks more detailed' is not enough: the face, shoulder construction, shield curvature, sword volume, and grip must visibly improve under matched before/after framing.
